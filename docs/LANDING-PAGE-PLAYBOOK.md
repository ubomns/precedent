# Landing Page Conversion Playbook — Precedent

> Section-by-section architecture and copy guidance extracted from the brand strategy playbook. Use this as the source of truth for the redesigned `/` route.

---

## Page Architecture (in order)

### 1. Hero

**Goal**: name the pain, deliver the differentiator, and convert immediately.

| Element | Copy / Guidance |
|---|---|
| Headline | "The BRD shouldn't be the hardest part of your job." |
| Sub-headline | "Precedent turns a structured project brief into a near-complete Business Requirements Document — 12 sections, schema-driven, with a built-in critic agent that catches what a second reviewer would catch. Validated with practising BAs in regulated environments." |
| Primary CTA | "Generate my BRD — free to try →" |
| Secondary CTA | "Download a sample BRD →" |

**Why it works**: the headline names the pain without drama. The sub-headline combines proof, differentiation, and audience in one breath. "Validated with practising BAs in regulated environments" is the strongest trust signal — move it from hidden case study to hero.

---

### 2. The Problem (The BA's Real Day)

**Goal**: make the visitor feel understood before presenting the solution.

**Copy**:

> You know what a good BRD looks like. You've written dozens of them. The problem isn't skill — it's the structural writing that has to happen before you get to the thinking that actually requires you.
>
> By the time you've drafted the Executive Summary, the Scope boundaries, the Stakeholder matrix, and the Functional Requirements table, the document is six pages long and you haven't written a single sentence that required your judgment yet.
>
> Precedent handles the structure. You handle the thinking.

---

### 3. How It Works (3 Steps)

| Step | Title | Body |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fill in the brief | Enter what only you know: the project objective, stakeholders, scope boundaries, target users, and regulatory context. No guessing, no invented context. |
| 2 | Precedent drafts 12 sections | A drafter agent generates each BRD section against a typed schema. An independent critic agent reviews every section and surfaces issues by severity — high, medium, and low. |
| 3 | Export to Word | Download a fully formatted .docx that matches standard enterprise BRD conventions — heading styles, numbered requirements, RACI tables, and all 12 sections ready for your team's review. |

---

### 4. The 12 Sections (Show the Output)

**Goal**: buyers want to know exactly what they get. Listing the sections doubles as a feature list and trust signal.

**Copy**:

> Every generated BRD includes:
>
> 01 Executive Summary  
> 02 Business Objective  
> 03 Scope (in / out)  
> 04 Stakeholders & RACI  
> 05 Functional Requirements  
> 06 Non-Functional Requirements  
> 07 Assumptions  
> 08 Constraints  
> 09 Acceptance Criteria  
> 10 User Access Management  
> 11 Security Controls  
> 12 Benefits Realisation  
>
> All 12 sections are generated against a typed schema. The output is structured, not prose soup.

---

### 5. Sample Output (Critical)

**Goal**: remove trust friction by letting buyers preview the actual deliverable.

**Required actions**:

- Generate one complete BRD using a realistic but fictional project brief (e.g., "A customer portal for a regional insurance company to manage policy renewals and claims online").
- Download the Word export, clean it up slightly.
- Publish it as a free download on the landing page.
- Include a screenshot or inline preview of the Word export.

**Copy anchor**:

> A BA cannot trust a tool they cannot preview. Download a sample BRD to see the structure, numbering, and formatting before you commit.

---

### 6. Who It's For

**Goal**: qualify the audience and reinforce the regulated-environment moat.

**Copy**:

> Precedent is built for Business Analysts working in regulated environments — banking, insurance, healthcare, public sector, and enterprise IT — where a BRD is not optional and the format is not negotiable.
>
> If your team has a house template, Precedent's default Word output follows standard enterprise conventions. Template upload (so it renders directly into your team's format) is on the roadmap for v1.1.
>
> If you work independently, the default template is clean, numbered, and ready to submit.

## Positioning Statement

Use in About / Services sections or as a strapline:

> Most AI writing tools produce text. Precedent produces a BRD — schema-validated, 12 sections, enterprise-flavoured by default, with Security Controls, UAM, and Benefits Realisation as first-class outputs rather than afterthoughts. Built for environments where the format is not optional.

---

## Conversion Principles

- Every word pulls toward a decision or pushes away. There is no neutral copy.
- Lead with the regulated-environment angle — it is the moat.
- Primary CTAs must be specific and active, not passive ("Get in touch").
- Social proof and sample output should be visible before the final CTA.
- Copy should speak to buyers who need things built, not builders who make things.
